Describe it really simple

5 min

Imagine you meet an alien. They can speak your language, but their vocabulary is scarce. They do not know Threadbare and what a StoryQuest is.

How would you describe your StoryQuest to them in very few words - but still capture its essence?

If time allows, role-play it: pair up with someone who hasn't playtested your StoryQuest (so it's a mystery to them) and try to convey what it's about. The "alien" can reply only with nods and simple words - it's a fun test of how clearly your idea comes across.

What's a pitch?

5 min

A pitch is a short presentation that helps people quickly understand an idea. It's a powerful tool for getting potential investors, clients, and players excited about your game.

There's no single standard for what a video game pitch must include, but some common key points are:

  • Genre, story, and core mechanics of the game.
  • Target demographic.
  • Unique selling points.
  • Concept art, images, and a playthrough demo of the prototype.

A traditional game pitch is about getting people excited and clearly, efficiently communicating the core gameplay and what makes the game unique.

The final countdown

optional

The pitch needs to be catchy, and your team should give it real attention - but most importantly, you need to give closure to your prototype.

What's the last thing you want to polish before presenting your StoryQuest? Talk with your teammates and decide what's missing or needs fixing ASAP.

This is your last shot to polish the prototype - don't create or add anything from scratch. Everything needs closure, and nothing has to be perfect: be mindful about what can stay a (functional) draft and what really needs attention.

Share

Get it to the outside

5 min

Every team is heads-down on their own work - and no one will see your pitch until the last session. So take it outside your cohort:

  • Share your pitch with people you know - family, friends, anyone outside the program.
  • Ask for feedback: would they play your StoryQuest? What would they suggest you emphasize to make the pitch more appealing?

This is about playtesting your pitch, not the prototype itself. If you have time outside the session, let people hear the pitch and then play the StoryQuest - do they line up? Does the pitch really highlight what's unique or fun?
