Chicken or egg?

5 min

Games are art, and art has many ways of coming into existence. So where would you start?

  • Would you start with the gameplay?
  • Maybe the story?
  • Or something else entirely?

All of these pieces (and more) will coexist - but you have to start somewhere.

You already know what Threadbare's lore and StoryQuests are about, and you've built within Godot. With those two things in mind, take a second to think about where your starting point could be. You might help a teammate see theirs, and they might help you see yours.

Create

All hands on deck

10 min

You're going to start putting together your StoryQuest now. For that, you need a bigger team - 4-5 members max.

  • Your facilitator may use a team generator to find your team. You can also ask to stay with a teammate from a previous session - talk to your facilitator.
  • While that happens, go back to your Brainstorm #1 idea from Session 2 - your individual StoryQuest idea - and let it inspire you.

This is the team you'll create your StoryQuest with, so it's worth a good start.

Create

Fun stuff to have

10 min

With your team, play the mini-demo scenes in Threadbare Gameplay Elements. Go through them so you get a feel for what your StoryQuest could include - player abilities, puzzle objects, and enemies.

You'll mod everything you see to fit your StoryQuest, so these aren't limitations - they're a starting menu.

There's a lot to explore on the Gameplay Elements page - and you will explore it all eventually. For now, talk with your teammates and split up so each of you tries a different one, then compare notes.

Brainstorm #2 (team!)

30 min

Time for another brainstorm - but this time with your team. The result of this one is your team's StoryQuest idea.

  • Get together in the same place (for example, a virtual breakout room).
  • Use the same tldraw StoryQuest Brainstorming Template you used in Brainstorm #1. Duplicate the template so your team has its own copy.
Open: StoryQuest Brainstorming Template (tldraw) ↗

How to pace it

  • 5 min - ideate and write in each category: Location, Character, Scene/Scenario.
  • 5 min - individually pick your favorite sticky notes from each category and build your own StoryQuest idea.
  • 10 min - each member shares their idea, then the team debates which one to develop. You can mix parts from different ideas, or draw from Brainstorm #1.

You know more about Godot now - from the challenges and the Gameplay Elements - so you have extra things to throw into the mix. That doesn't make this any less of a Blue Sky Brainstorming: there's still a lot for you to learn, so dream big.

Share

Congrats!

5 min

Now you have your team's StoryQuest idea! Things might shift while you build the prototype, but this is the idea you'll start from. Share with the cohort what you and your team came up with.

Give feedback: compliment, coach, encourage

  • Compliment: "What I like about your idea is…"
  • Coach: "I think you could reuse this asset in…"
  • Encourage: "I see a lot of potential for…"

When you share, you'll hear different voices on how cool - and how feasible - your StoryQuest could be. Take it all in; it's a gift for your idea.
