What makes a game good? What makes it great?

A build session - warm up by playing like a designer, then get back to developing your StoryQuest with your team.

60 minutes Build session All hands on deck

This is a recurring build session (“All Hands on Deck”), used in Sessions 10, 12, 13, 15, and 16. Same rhythm each time: warm up, build, wrap up.

Play

Play as a designer

5 min

Before you get back to your StoryQuest, choose one game from the list and play it. Just play, and note anything you find unique or interesting in its design or gameplay.

  • Empty ↗
  • How to Crab to Zero ↗
  • Big-snek ↗
  • AfterMount ↗

More options your facilitator can point you to: Neon Swing, Browser Whales, Lock Picks, We Become What We Hold, You Are a Circle.

Playing these is the ideal warm-up - but if you're in a real time crunch, it's OK to skip. Never underestimate it, though: as you play, notice how the game feels - fun, surprising, satisfying, confusing, frustrating, or just unusual. Those reactions can feed your own StoryQuest.

Play

Where's the juiciness?

5 min

Share what you noticed with your team:

  • What stood out as interesting or unique about its design or gameplay?
  • What felt fun or satisfying?
  • What surprised or frustrated you?
  • Anything you'd change about the gameplay - or borrow?

There are no wrong answers here - just share what you noticed. Think as both a player and a game maker. You don't need to copy anything; simply experiencing new gameplay loops and clever mechanics gives you ideas to try or avoid.

Create

Back to building!

45 min

Check your GDD and Project Board and decide what you're going to work on today.

  • Pick a task or area of work.
  • Communicate with your team about who's doing what.
  • You won't know every step before you start - try, test, and adjust as you go.

A lot of what you're trying may be new or difficult, so it's fine if the plan or a task shifts once you start. If it gets too complicated, go back to drafting a problem decomposition.

Reflect

Before you go

5 min

Take a few minutes to wrap up and make your next steps clear:

  • Update your Project Board so it reflects any status changes, new tasks, or dependencies you found.
  • Let your team know where you're at with your task - and check whether anyone is waiting on you, or you on them.
  • Leave yourself something clear to pick up next session.

If you finished something, ask what the next logical thing to tackle is. If you didn't finish, decide whether continuing is your best next step, or whether something more crucial should come first - you can leave today's work as a draft and note that on the board.

Reflect

A little organization now makes it much easier to jump back into the work next time.
