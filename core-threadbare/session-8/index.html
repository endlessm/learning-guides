What area of game making calls you?

5 min

Game making is a multidisciplinary endeavor - there are many fields of work and many tasks up for grabs. So think about a video game you like:

  • What's one thing you would have liked to create in it?
  • If it was something like sound, do you see yourself making SFX for your StoryQuest?

Keep this in mind when you assign issues on the Project Board to yourself or others.

Your Project Board will keep filling up. The idea is that each team member brings their own skills to the table - and grows along the way.

Create

Start with the unknown

10 min

Before you dive back into Godot, check your GDD and look for a big unknown - something you expect will be difficult to make. Choose one:

  • A new mechanic or gameplay element you need to build from scratch.
  • A mechanic or feature you're not yet sure how to build with.
  • Something the rest of your StoryQuest will likely depend on.

If there's something like this in your plan, consider starting there. But if you'd rather start with something simple you already know how to do, that's OK too.

This isn't a rule - it's just worth finding out early whether a key part of your StoryQuest is possible in the time you have. You don't need to fully finish and polish it, but working on it long enough to feel more comfortable goes a long way.

Create

Problem decomposition

10 min

Any game component is more complex to create than it first seems. When you're faced with a big challenge like your "unknown," there's a technique that helps: problem decomposition - breaking one big task into smaller, simpler ones.

  • Check the example: a task divided into smaller tasks, each simpler than the last, until a big challenge starts to feel feasible.
  • Take the Big Unknown you want to develop first, and think with your team about the tasks or steps you'll need.
  • Write those tasks or steps down as a concept map.

In the example, you won't see finished tasks - you'll see the kinds of questions a team might ask themselves in order to build something like a moving platform. Problem decomposition is all about asking the right questions.

Create

Try it!

30 min

Select just one task from your big unknown, based on what you mapped out. Then go to Godot and, with your teammates, use your intuition and technical skills to finish that one task.

Keep the goal of the task in mind as you build. Use the Setting Up a New Scene from Scratch guide to support your process - and if you need more material, browse the Guide Hub.

…and in the Project Board

5 min

Your task

  • 1Place the subtasks you drafted into the Todo category on your Project Board.
  • 2Mark as completed the one subtask you built in Godot today.
  • 3When you deliver the Project Board, answer in the submission: was the task small and easy enough to complete, or would it have been better to divide it further?

This is the second task you deliver on the Platform - your Project Board, a little more populated than before.
