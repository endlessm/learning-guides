How do you organize a project?

5 min

Share with your classmates how you keep your tasks and projects organized - from school or even in your personal life.

  • Do you use sticky notes to remind yourself of the steps to finish something?
  • Do you set a deadline for a task?
  • Do you use a spreadsheet or a Kanban board to keep track?

Organizing a project looks different for everyone, but every project needs some kind of organizing tool. In this program we'll use two specific - and very different - ones: a Game Design Document and a Project Board.

Meet the GDD

5 min

A GDD, or Game Design Document, is a tool game makers use to give shape to a project. It's flexible - nothing is set in stone.

  • Take the GDD Template and make one copy per team. Whoever makes it shares it with their teammates.
  • Read the document and ask questions about any sections or concepts you'd like to go over before jumping in.
Open: GDD Template ↗

Real GDDs from popular games are hard to find - they're intellectual property. This template has the kinds of categories a professional studio's GDD might include, just shorter and lighter.

Start planning

25 min

Take the StoryQuest idea your team landed on in Session 6 and use your new GDD to expand it. Talk with your teammates and fill in the sections together.

A GDD is flexible because when you're planning, there's still a lot you don't know. As you try to complete a section, you might realize something needs to change - that's exactly what it's for.

As you build in Godot, you might find a feature takes far longer than expected and you need a workaround. That's fine - the GDD is a living document, and it will change alongside your development.

Create

Your new best friend

5 min

Your GDD is your first tool - think of it as your compass. But how do you organize the work to actually build what's in it? That's where a Project Board comes in.

Use the Welcome to your StoryQuest Project Board guide to access a pre-made board that shows how you could organize your work, and read it to learn how to use the board.

The "Start here!" cards on the board are reference cards, not work to complete. Each shows the kinds of work that might fall under a category, with fictional example issues - they're examples, not a checklist.

Create

Customize the board!

25 min

Now make your two tools talk to each other:

  • Go back to your GDD and look at a section like StoryQuest Overview. When it asks "Who or what is your character?", that's a clue.
  • Is there a "character" category on your Project Board? If so, create an actual issue for the character work and drop it in the Todo list.
  • Create as many issues as you need, and choose an owner for each one.

As you develop, you'll keep adding issues - so talk to each other about who's doing what, and keep the board updated (just like the GDD). That way, if someone misses a session, they can come back and see exactly what changed and how it affects their work.
